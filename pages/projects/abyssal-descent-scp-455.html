Abyssal Descent: SCP-455

Abyssal Descent: SCP-455 is an university project made by a big team composed of 3 different masters courses, Software Engineering (programming), Visual Arts (visuals) and Multimedia (sound design), where we had to create a game from scratch. Abyssal Descent: SCP-455 is a 3D horror VR experience inside the world of SCP-455 where the player will be put in the shoes of a SCP Foundation agent, sent to investigate a mysterious ship abandoned at sea. As you explore the ship, you will uncover the dark secrets that lie within, facing horrors as you descend into the abyss.

My team was tasked with the game's sound design. A new challenge to tackle, as none of us had done anything horror related prior. But a challenge we happily went into face on. We had to first wait the first concept and demo shots from our programmers, as well as the first concept art from the artists so we could work on an atmosphere that would go hand in hand with the rest that the player would be experiencing. What we got was this solitary cargo ship in the middle of the ocean with a very rusty and decayed appearance with phantasmagoric vibes all around.

With the first drafts of the whole vibe, we got into work to make this project sound like something!

The Music

For the music, Amir produced some amazing and very eery generative music that quickly gives a mysterious and an ominous presence to the dark and claustrophobic corridors of the ship.

The Ambience

We got the environment sounds from free online libraries and tuned them for our needs so they would fit perfectly into the places on the game they would be used.

We created most of the VFX sounds by ourselves. In our sound room we had a lot of metallic objects, like stands and cabinets, so we used those as percussion instruments and made beautiful sounds for our in-game actions and events. A good example of it is the step sounds, which I made by laying a chair on the ground and wall walking on a metal cabinet, which made quite a echoey metal clank.

The Voicelines

We had to add some voice lines, which I did by recording ourselves in our studio mic and adding post processing to the speech. It was pretty cool how I was able to easily distort and add white noise and interference to the voice so it sounds like its coming from a bad walkie-talkie and losing signal while the voice line plays. The static would automatically decline whenever the voice was detected, so the speech would be understandable and it would give the interference effect of higher noise when no real audio is being sent through the network.

The Ghosts

The ghosts were easily my favorite part to make, which without any bias at all, I think they came out pretty well.

The design for the ghosts VFX was also inspired by generative music, since the randomness by nature can give it a pretty eery feeling. The idea would be that the ghosts would sound like the collective souls from all of the souls they had consumed, sounding like muffled, chaotic and desperate whispers that would intensify and increase in number the closer it got.

In order to achieve the effect I recorded around 30 voice lines, some whispering and some more aggressive. Then I put them into FMOD and created a sound system that would play an higher number of overlapped voicelines if the player was closer to the ghosts. This was more recoding work than it was technical, but the final effect came out pretty cool and gave a lot of life to the enemies in the game.

Introspection

The game itself was pretty basic and honestly something more akin to a walking experience than an actually fully fledged game. But the whole feeling of playing it and experiencing it ended up being pretty cool once the sound and the visuals joined forces to bring the whole atmosphere to the player.

Overall, it was a cool project to work on and enabled us to learn even more about generative audio and how it can be used to bring whole new types of experiences to the audience.
